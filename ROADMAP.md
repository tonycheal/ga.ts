## The big shift: Lie, not just CGA

`LIESPHERE.md` records the main finding from this session: Apollonius's `lcp` /
`LCP` records already *are* Lie sphere vectors in `R(3,2)`, and the boolean
"flip" flags are the sign of the oriented-radius coordinate. That answers the
open question about how flags relate to sphere orientation, and it changes the
target algebra for the Apollonius work from CGA `R(3,1)` to Lie `R(3,2)`.

CGA does not become wrong — it stays the right tool for unoriented incidence,
and Lie contains it (`toCGA` in `lie2d.ts` is just "drop `er`"). But the
Apollonius solver wants the oriented one. See the next section for where that
division of labour turns out to be permanent rather than transitional.

## What LSG does and does not give you

Settled 2026-09-03, and it shapes the API. All of this is checked.

**Constraints are vectors.** Every condition you can impose on the answer is
"be orthogonal to this vector `V`", and `V`'s own norm says which kind:

| `V` | constraint |
|---|---|
| null (`V·V = 0`) | be tangent to this cycle |
| spacelike | meet it at angle θ, where `sin θ = \|V\| / r` |
| `V` = infinity | the answer must be a line / plane |
| the radius vector | the answer must have radius r |

`makeCoefficients(S, θ)` in Apollonius's `NewTanCode.ts` already builds exactly
this vector: `S` with its `er` coordinate scaled by `cos θ`. Tangency is not
the general case with flags bolted on — it is the *special* case where the
constraint vector happens to lie on the quadric.

**LSG has no sub-dimensional rounds, and this is structural.** Blend two null
vectors: `(λA + μB)² = 2λμ (A·B)`. So the joining line stays on the quadric
only when `A·B = 0` — only when the two spheres are *tangent*. Lines on the Lie
quadric are pencils of mutually tangent spheres (contact elements), never
pencils through a common circle.

Consequences:

- a circle in 3D is **not** an LSG object;
- **LSG cannot intersect two circles** — that is a point pair, a grade-2 CGA
  blade, and Apollonius PDT certainly constructs those.

So the architecture is not "LSG replaces CGA". It is **LSG for the tangency
solver, CGA for incidence and intersection**, over the same numbers, converting
by adding or dropping `er`. That promotes "extract `cga2d.ts`" from tidying to
load-bearing.

**Build cost doubles per dimension and then some.** Measured:

| n | algebra | basis | build |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | `R(3,2)` | 32 | 26 ms |
| 3 | `R(4,2)` | 64 | 136 ms |
| 4 | `R(5,2)` | 128 | 2.6 s |
| 5 | `R(6,2)` | 256 | 71 s |

`ga.ts` builds all five Cayley tables eagerly at construction. Fine for n=2 and
n=3, which is all Apollonius needs, but **cache the algebra per dimension in a
module-level `Map<number, Algebra>`** and never construct inside a solver call.
"Any dimension" has a hard ceiling around n=4.

## The interface — settled 2026-09-03

Everything here is checked; see the working notes in `LIESPHERE.md` for the
underlying identities.

### Two families, not one

Null vectors are **objects**. Non-null vectors are **mirrors**. The group is
generated by reflections in the non-null ones (Cartan–Dieudonné), so the API
divides accordingly:

```ts
// find something unknown
L.solve([ L.touch(c1), L.touch(c2).at(30), L.through(p) ])

// move something known
L.invert(X, inCycle)     // circle -> inversion, line -> reflection: ONE call
L.offset(X, d)           // CCD, LLD, and point -> circle of radius d
L.translate(X, v)        // PPV
```

**Constraints are per object, never per combination.** `L.touch(c1)` names one
object and one relation. The moment a combination label like `LCP` appears in a
signature, the enum is back wearing a nicer coat — `LCP` is what the *UI* knows
about the click sequence, and it must never reach the solver.

### `invert` and `reflect` are the same operation

You cannot sandwich by a cycle in LSG: every cycle is null, so none has an
inverse (`ga.ts` throws). What you sandwich by is the **90° constraint vector**
— the cycle with `er` zeroed — which is spacelike with norm² = r².

So the vector meaning "be orthogonal to C" is the same vector that generates
"invert in C". Not a coincidence: inversion in C fixes exactly the circles
orthogonal to C. For a line that vector has norm² = 1 and the identical formula
gives ordinary reflection.

Verified: `(3,0)` inverts in the unit circle to `(1/3, 0)` and reflects in
`x = 1` to `(-1, 0)`, from one call. A line not through the centre inverts to a
circle through the centre. And `circle(0,0,2)` comes back as `r = -0.5` —
**inversion reverses orientation, and LSG records it.** CGA would lose that.

### `P · L` is the signed distance from a point to a line

Exact, not an approximation. Which makes "equidistant from two lines" the
linear condition `P · (L1 − L2) = 0`, so the bisector is a subtraction plus a
normalise — and **the orientation flag picks which of the two bisectors**
(0° and 45° give 22.5° or 112.5°, by reversing one input).

### What LSG does *not* touch

An audit of the 51 `case` labels in Apollonius's `Recalculate.ts` (quick read,
not exhaustive):

| family | n | what it is | LSG? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `L…` lines | 14 | tangents, perpendiculars, parallels | yes |
| `P…` points | 8 | intersections, midpoints, on-curve | partly |
| `C…` circles | 6 | tangent circles, offsets | yes |
| `D…` distances | 6 | `DCC` is `r2 - r1` | no |
| `M…` measures | 6 | sets `text`, `start`, `end`, `offset` | no |
| `I…` ink | 4 | arcs, rendering geometry | no |
| `A…` angles | 3 | `ALL` is complex multiplication of normals | no |
| `S…`, `V…` | 4 | `VDD` is an assignment | no |

Roughly **twenty** are genuine tangency/incidence constructions that collapse.
The other thirty-one are arithmetic on scalars or drawing furniture, and stay
exactly as they are. Do not oversell the collapse.

Evidence that the twenty really are one thing: `CCD` and `LLD` are the *same
line of code* (`block.c += arg2.c`) under two labels — and that operation is
the Laguerre shear, which moves circles and lines together in one step.

### Constructions become data, not labels

The ceiling on what a user can draw is currently the length of a `switch`
statement, and it only moves when Tony ships. The fix is already half-written:

```ts
block.flips: boolean[]     ->     block.angles: number[]
//           false, true                        0, 180
```

`NewTanCode.makeCoefficients` already takes a continuous angle and already has
the `cos` behind it. Once angles are data, "twist this tangent to 30°" is a
drag that edits a number, with the same solver running, and users can build
constructions nobody enumerated.

### Let the cursor disambiguate, and delete the flags from the UI

There are only eight orientation patterns and each solve is a small nullspace
plus a quadratic. So **solve all eight and rank by distance to the cursor.**

```
power    = -2 (X · P)          // P = cursor as a point-cycle; = d² - r²
distance = |power| / (d + r)   // = |d - r|, exact
```

Verified: three cursor positions each select the geometrically right circle.
This inverts the interaction — the user drags and the answer follows, rather
than picking a construction and then toggling flags until it looks right. Flags
become a **readout** ("solution 3 of 8"), derived from what the user chose, and
kept in the file format for reconstruction. Patterns with no real solution
simply do not appear in the ranking.

### Keep degenerate results complex, but do not draw them

A CGA point pair does not vanish when two circles separate — its square goes
negative and it becomes an imaginary pair, still a perfectly good multivector.
Measured on two unit circles as the second slides away: `+3.75, +15.75, 0.00`
at tangency, then `-56.25, -768.00`. Never a `NaN`.

Use that for **branch continuity while dragging**: keeping the value complex
through a degeneracy stops the two solutions swapping places, so the answer
under the cursor stays the answer under the cursor. Render nothing while the
square is negative. The LSG equivalent is `B² - 4AC < 0` at the quadric step.

## Next steps, in order

### 1. `lsg.ts` — the solver  *(the main event)*

Numbers in, numbers out, algebra hidden. Dimension-generic, cached per `n`:

```ts
const L = lsg(2);                    // or lsg(3)

const c1 = L.sphere([0, 0], 5);      // signed radius carries orientation
const c2 = L.sphere([9, 0], -3);
const l1 = L.plane([0, 1], 4);       // line in 2D, plane in 3D
const p  = L.point([2, 7]);

L.solve([ L.tangent(c1), L.tangent(c2), L.through(p) ]);
L.solve([ L.atAngle(l1, 90), L.through(p), L.flat() ]);
```

See "The interface" above for the full API shape, the transform family, and
the cursor-disambiguation model.

Design rules, each earned above:

- `tangent`, `atAngle`, `through`, `flat`, `withRadius` all return the **same
  type** — a constraint vector. `solve` takes a list of them. No `flags`
  array, no per-case entry point.
- `solve` takes **n+1 constraints** and returns up to 2 solutions. Orientation
  is chosen by the sign of the radius on the inputs.
- **Stay homogeneous inside.** Only `decode` divides by `eo`, and it decides
  line-versus-very-large-circle against a tolerance the *caller* supplies —
  that is a modelling decision about drawing extent, not a numerical accident.
  The old "change the question at the limit" workaround is not needed.
- **Port the stable quadratic from `NewTanCode.ts` verbatim.** Its two roots
  both use `+√D`, computing `1/t₋` via `t₊t₋ = c/a` to dodge catastrophic
  cancellation. It looks like a copy-paste slip and is not; do not "tidy" it.
- Expose `toCGA` / `fromCGA` as first-class, given the intersection story.

Solution counts fall out of the dimension arithmetic: n+3 homogeneous numbers,
n+1 constraints, sign patterns pairing up — **8 circles tangent to 3 circles,
16 spheres tangent to 4 spheres.** Both classical. Use them as tests.

Deliverables: `lsg.ts`, `test-lsg.ts` — and **delete `visualiser/solver.js`**, the
stopgap that exists only so the visualiser has something to drive it, pointing
`visualiser/programs.js` at the real solver instead. Build 2D end-to-end and check it against
`tan3G` *before* generalising the dimension — the generic version is more
convincing once there is something to check it against.

**Settled 2026-09-06: solve it with GA operations, not elimination.** This was
listed here as an open question and it turned out to be the answer to the
root-choosing problem as well, so it is written up in full under "Choosing the
root" below. In short: the constraint set `{X : X · Sᵢ = 0}` is the orthogonal
complement of `S₁ ∧ S₂ ∧ S₃` — one bivector `B`, the **contact blade** — and
the two answers are the eigenvectors of `M = B / √(B·B)`, which squares to 1.
No elimination, no pivoting, and no ± flag on a discriminant. Implemented in
`visualiser/solver.js`; port it to `lsg.ts` as-is.

Note the one place the metric has to be put in by hand: `dual` in `ga.ts` is
the metric-free complement, so the constraints must be index-lowered
(`Sⱼ ↦ Σ (eⱼ · S) eⱼ`) before wedging. Doing it the other way round silently
gives a bivector that is not the pencil at all.

## Choosing the root — settled 2026-09-06

This is the thing `tan3G` does with `IF flags[2] THEN s = -s`: a bare sign flag
on a square root, set once at construction from the click geometry and never
questioned again. It works in practice, and it has a known failure — push a
solution through the line case (radius through infinity) and the two answers
change places, so the branch the user picked comes back on the other side.

**Why it happens.** `s = √(B² − 4AC)` is a positive number, and the ± chooses
between two roots of a quadratic written in a *frame* — the nullspace basis
`{a, b}` that elimination happened to produce. Which columns get pivoted on
changes as the configuration moves, so the frame changes discontinuously, and
with it the meaning of "the + root". The sign flag is then correcting for an
accident of the arithmetic rather than naming anything geometric.

**The fix is to stop having a frame.** The pencil of candidate answers is a
plane, and that plane is a single bivector:

```
    B  =  complement( Ŝ₁ ∧ Ŝ₂ ∧ … ∧ Ŝₙ₊₁ )        Ŝ = S with its index lowered
    M  =  B / √(B·B)                              so that  M*M = 1
    X± =  ½ (v ± M v)                             v = any probe projected into B
```

`M` is an involution of the pencil and `X±` are its two eigenvectors. Every
step is a continuous function of the constraint list, so `X₊` stays `X₊`. The
only square root taken is `√(B·B)`, which is a **positive scalar belonging to
the blade** — not a ± choice belonging to a coordinate system. And nothing
anywhere divides by `eo`, which is exactly why the line case stops being a
special point.

The labelling is then fixed by the **order and orientation of the constraint
list** — the user's click order and flip flags — and by nothing else. Reverse
two constraints and `X₊`/`X₋` swap, as they should. Move the geometry and they
never do.

**Checked**, 721 frames of the "drag" configuration (two unit circles, a third
swept through them), measuring the solutions in the sign-free chart
`(x/r, y/r, 1/r, d/r)` which stays finite through the line case:

| ordering | roughness Σ\|2nd diff\| | discontinuities |
|---|---|---|
| pivoted elimination + ± flag | 4.22 / 4.11 | a jump of 2.0 at the line case |
| contact blade | 0.118 / 0.118 | none |

and the curvature `1/r` of one branch either side of the line case:

```
  y = -0.100  -0.050  -0.025   0.000   0.025   0.050   0.100
       .0480   .0248   .0125   .0000  -.0128  -.0259  -.0531
```

— straight through zero. That *is* pushing through infinity: the circle grows,
becomes a line, and comes back curving the other way with the opposite
orientation. The radius sign flip is the correct answer, not a swap. The old
ordering gives `.0480 .0248 .0125 0 -.0064 -.0259 -.0531` on one branch and a
mirrored bounce on the other.

The classical counts are unchanged: 8 circles tangent to 3 circles, 16 spheres
tangent to 4 spheres, both re-checked against the new solver.

**What this leaves for the flags.** Orientation flags still choose which of the
`2ⁿ⁺¹` patterns you are solving — that is real geometric data and it stays. The
*root* flag is what disappears: there is no longer anything for it to select
that the constraint list does not already say. Which is a prediction about
Apollonius, and it holds: for `CLLD` (radius given, tangent to two lines) the
pencil meets the quadric at the answer **and at the point at infinity**, so
there is exactly one circle per corner and no second root at all. `tan2GL`
being linear is not an implementation shortcut — it is the geometry — and the
original PDT's habit of always clearing the flag on the Distance is right.

### 2. The parallel experiment against Apollonius

Tony's stated goal: run the GA formulation alongside the current `tan2G` /
`tan3G` and compare. Plan:

- Keep it **in this repo**, not in Apollonius. Commit `4258feb` in
  `~/Dev/apollonius` deliberately removed `ts-geometric-algebra` and

- Vendor a copy of `TanCode.ts` / `NewTanCode.ts` into a `compare/` directory
  (they have no imports, so this is a straight copy) and write a harness that
  runs both solvers over randomised and hand-picked configurations, comparing:
  **agreement** (do they find the same cycles?), **coverage** (does either miss
  solutions the other finds?), **conditioning** (near-degenerate inputs: nearly
  parallel lines, nearly equal circles, tangency at the input), and **speed**.
- Report as a table, not prose. The interesting result is most likely
  *robustness at degeneracies*, where `tan3G` has explicit `d === 0`,
  `l === 0`, `C !== 0` branches and the Lie version should have none.
- Only after that: decide whether Apollonius adopts it, and how (see 3).

### 3. Upgrade implications for Apollonius

If the comparison goes the GA way, the migration is smaller than it looks
because the data structures already match:

- `lcp` ↔ Lie vector is a five-field relabel (table in `LIESPHERE.md`), so
  Apollonius's stored geometry does not have to change at all — only the
  solver behind `callTan2` / `callTan3`.
- The `newTanCode` boolean already switches solvers at those two call sites.
  A third option is a one-line change, which makes A/B testing inside the
  live app cheap.
- **Fix the return type as part of the move.** `tan2G`/`tan3G` return `quad`
  (orientation dropped, and `c` meaning radius rather than the input's
  quadratic term — see `LIESPHERE.md`). A Lie solver naturally returns a full
  oriented cycle; returning `lcp` instead of `quad` removes a real asymmetry
  and probably some downstream sign handling.
- The `angles` parameter generalises for free: `makeCoefficients` already
  takes a continuous angle, and `cosAngle` in `lie2d.ts` is the same quantity.
  "Circle tangent to these three" and "circle meeting these three at 30°"
  become the same call.

### 4. 3D  *(the claim is proved; the solver is not)*

Done, and it cost what it was supposed to. `lie3d.ts` is `lie2d.ts` with `e3`
inserted — one extra row and column in the transform matrix — and
`test-lie3d.ts` is a line-by-line mirror that passed 71/71 first run. No
special case appeared. The visualiser's solver needed one change to go
dimension-generic: `basis` became a parameter.

Spheres tangent to four spheres works and gives **16** distinct answers from
2⁴ sign patterns, as the dimension arithmetic predicted. What does *not* exist
yet is `lsg.ts` itself — the 3D path currently runs through

The parallel CGA line (`R(4,1)`) should get the same treatment — the existing
`test-cga2d.ts` setup should generalise to `test-cga3d.ts` with one extra
basis vector and no other changes.

### 5. Packaging (`npm`)

Tony's read — "fame rather than fortune", plenty of alternatives already —
is right, and it shapes the packaging decisions rather than arguing against
them. What that means concretely:

- **Name:** `ga.ts` is unclaimed on npm (checked 2026-09-02) and matches the
  repo. Take it. `@tonycheal/ga` is also free as a fallback.
- **Licence:** MIT. Nothing about a fame-not-fortune goal is served by a
  restrictive licence, and permissive licensing is what gets a library used.
- **Minimum viable package:** done as of `7a4f490` / `f94a4ec` — `package.json`
  with subpath `exports` for `./lie2d` and `./interpreter`, declarations, and a
  `prepare` script so a GitHub install builds itself. Still outstanding: a
  `README.md` that leads with a *worked example* (the Apollonius picture)
  rather than a feature list. It is currently three lines.
- **Tag before recommending an install.** `v0.1.0` sits on `7a4f490`, which
  predates the `./lie2d` export, so installs from that tag cannot reach the
  Lie layer at all.
- **What actually earns attention** is the differentiator, and it is not
  "another GA library": it is (a) the parent-algebra/transform basis-change
  machinery, which most libraries do not expose at all, and (b) Lie sphere
  geometry with oriented cycles, which almost none of them have. Lead with
  those. `ganja.js` owns the "pretty demos" ground; do not compete there.
- **Do not** publish before the interpreter's API is stable — a tagged
  template literal in the public surface is a compatibility commitment.

Sequence: 1 → 2 → 5 → 3, with 4 as a spike whenever it is useful. Packaging
before the Apollonius migration, because a published, versioned dependency is a
cleaner thing for Apollonius to consume than a relative path across `~/Dev`.
