Lie sphere geometry, without the algebra

Circles Are Ripples

Everything in one page of bullet points. No manifolds were harmed. Approved by two cats.

01

Start with the one idea

  • A circle is three numbers: x, y, r.
  • So stop thinking of a circle as a set of points. Think of it as one point in a space of all circles.
  • Every circle in the plane is a single dot in that space. A family of circles is a curve through it.
  • Now let r be allowed to go negative. Plus and minus are the two ways round. That is your flip flag — nothing more.

A circle of radius r is a ripple: drop a stone at (x, y), wait r seconds.

Radius is time. A positive radius is a ripple spreading outward; a negative one is a ripple closing back in.

02

Why one minus sign changes everything

  • Two circles touch when the distance between their centres equals the difference of their radii.
  • Written out, that is:
Δx² + Δy² − Δr² = 0
  • Ordinary distance in three dimensions would be Δx² + Δy² + Δr². This is the same thing with a minus sign on the radius.
  • A minus sign in a distance formula is not a small thing. It is the geometry of special relativity.
  • Which means, said physically: two circles are tangent exactly when one stone's ripple arrives at the other stone's splash.
  • Tangency is the question “can these two events see each other by light?”
03

One number tells you everything about a pair

  • Take any two circles and combine them into a single number — the dot product, written X · Y.
  • It is exactly the spacetime gap between the two splashes. Its sign is the whole story:
X · Y > 0
one is inside the other. Nested. Never touching.
X · Y = 0
they touch. On the light cone.
X · Y < 0
they cross, or sit apart.

“Inside” and “outside” is not a test you have to write. It is the sign of a dot product.

The thing you have been case-analysing for thirty years is the causal structure of a ripple. Nested circles are the pair of events where one could have caused the other.

04

Why five numbers, not three

  • You want lines and points in the club too:
    • a point is a circle of radius zero;
    • a line is a circle of infinite radius.
  • Infinity is awkward with three plain coordinates. You already know the fix — you shipped it in Euclid. Homogeneous coordinates.
  • Same move: a 3D point became four numbers so that points at infinity stopped being a special case. A circle's three numbers become five, and lines stop being a special case.
  • The books keep coming out even: five numbers, minus one for overall scale, minus one for the “must be a ripple” constraint, leaves the three you started with.

Your lcp record is those five numbers.

You derived {a, bx, by, c, k} yourself, by hand, because it made the algebra tidy — and landed exactly on the standard coordinates. That is why every one of them satisfies the ripple condition automatically. It was never luck.

05

Every question you can ask is a vector

  • This is the part that collapses the whole zoo. Every constraint is “be at right angles to this vector”. All of them.
  • And the vector's own length tells you which kind of constraint it is:
    • vector on the cone → “be tangent to this circle”
    • vector off the cone → “meet this circle at an angle” — and the angle is the length of the vector
    • the vector for infinity → “the answer must be a straight line”
    • a similar vector → “the answer must have radius 5”
  • There is no separate angle machinery. Tangency is not the ordinary case with flags bolted on — it is the special case, where the constraint vector happens to be a ripple itself.
  • So: stack up your vectors, solve, and the answers fall out. Perpendicular, tangent, through a point, at 30°, must-be-a-line — one function.

Eight tangent circles is not eight cases. It is eight patterns of plus and minus.

Three input circles, each of which can run either way round: 2 × 2 × 2. The branching in tan3G is a case analysis standing in for a sign.

06

Three dimensions costs one number

  • Spheres in space work the same way. A sphere is a ripple in a bigger pond.
  • Five numbers become six. Everything above holds word for word: the same touching rule, the same dot product, the same signs meaning nested / tangent / crossing.
  • Tested it — the 3D version was one extra row in a table and no new code at all.
  • This is the actual reason for doing any of it. Not elegance. The 3D solver is the 2D solver.
07

The honest bit

  • None of the mathematics is new. Sophus Lie worked it out in 1872. The Clifford-algebra version is well established. Inversive distance is Coxeter. It is a known, named, taught subject.
  • What is yours is that you rebuilt those five coordinates from scratch, without knowing they had a name, because the algebra pushed you there.
  • What may still be open is the engineering: the published geometric-algebra treatments of Apollonius are still done case by case. One uniform solver — stack of vectors, one quadratic, every case and all eight signs — does not obviously exist yet.
  • That is the part worth building, and it is a smaller and better claim than “new mathematics”.
